<h3>Welcome to the ROB 474/574 site!</h3>

This site holds the documentation and lab instructions for ROB 474/574 at the University of Michigan.

474/574 is a hands-on introduction to the design, modeling, control, and experimental evaluation of uncrewed aerial systems. Students study multirotor and fixed-wing aircraft from the propeller up: propulsion and power systems, airframe design and sizing, sensors and avionics, rigid-body dynamics, attitude and position control, state estimation, trajectory generation, and vision-based autonomy. Every analytical topic is paired with laboratory work on real hardware, so that students learn not only how to model an aerial vehicle but how to instrument it, measure its behavior, and reconcile measurement with theory.

The course is deliberately experimental in emphasis. Students characterize a propulsion system on a dynamometer, assemble and configure a flight vehicle, implement and tune a flight controller, evaluate closed-loop performance in a motion capture volume, and complete a self-directed final project culminating in a flight demonstration and technical presentation.
